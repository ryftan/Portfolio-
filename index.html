About

A bit about the work.

I'm Ryftan, a narrative designer and founder of Dusk Harbor — an independent practice built on the conviction that the best game stories are constructed, not written. Architecture first. Voice after.

My work starts where most writers stop: the frameworks, systems, and pipelines that make a narrative coherent at every level of production. I think in hierarchies — creative vision, world logic, character architecture, then dialogue.

Currently under contract with Olympos Games (Turkey) as Narrative Lead on Tides of Eternity, directing 2-3 other narrative designers off the frameworks I built for the project — a 14-person action-RPG roguelite targeting Steam in mid-2028.

How I actually work.

Before a single line of dialogue was written, Olympos Games needed a framework the whole narrative department could build on. This is a condensed look at what I designed: four governing pillars, and the 14-phase production pipeline that structures every document that follows.

P1
The World Remembers

Persistent consequence is the spine of the design. Player actions alter relationships, modify environments, and echo later — even the small ones. Nothing disappears completely.

P2
Emotional Ambiguity

No good/evil binaries. Characters carry understandable motivations, contradictions, and vulnerabilities — the player should genuinely debate their own choices afterward.

P3
Discovery Through Observation

The story rarely over-explains itself. Architecture, tone, and recurring symbols reveal information progressively — the player investigates, rather than gets told.

P4
Narrative Integration

Story isn't placed on top of gameplay — story is gameplay. Mechanics and progression systems are built to reinforce the same themes the narrative is carrying.

How I think

Most of narrative design happens before any dialogue gets written. The player builds the story in their own head, from what you leave for them to find.

The best characters don't explain themselves. They contradict themselves — and in the contradiction, they become real.

A world feels alive when every detail has a reason to exist, not when there's simply more of it.

I'm seventeen. I've already shipped narrative frameworks for a 14-person international studio. I'm not waiting for permission to do this work.

01
Core Pillar

Most major characters are not evil — conflict emerges from responsibility, fear, and exhaustion. Tragedy without villainy is a hard rule: even the game's central threat must never read as demonic or sadistic. It is nature reclaiming what was taken from it.

Core Narrative Pillars — 2.4 Tragedy Without Villainy
02
Environmental Design

The Hub should feel like controlled authority fraying at the edges — grand and deliberate, but something is wrong, and the player has to feel it without being told. Surfaces that should gleam are dulled. Rituals that should feel powerful feel mechanical.

Environmental & Atmospheric Documentation — Hub, Emotional Identity
03
Governing Principle

"The ocean should never feel conquered. Only momentarily understood." This line governs every creative decision in the game — the player is meant to finish reflective and uncertain, transformed by understanding rather than victory.

World-Building

Verdania's forest is written as beautiful and wounded at once — a lush, peaceful woodland where the details slowly betray the exploitation underneath: felled stumps stamped with council ownership, rivers running polluted from pipes hidden beneath the roots.

Rocky never speaks — the game is silent by design. His entire personality is written to live in reaction: a hesitation, a glance back, a tremble before a difficult jump. Brave without ever being boastful.
